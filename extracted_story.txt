Case ID: LS-047 — The Last Signal
Location: Advanced Research and Intelligence Laboratory (ARIL), a fictional university research facility.
Missing researcher: Dr. Arjun Raman, lead investigator of Project Phantom.
Project Phantom: A confidential research programme combining secure communication systems, intelligent surveillance, embedded sensor networks, and AI-based anomaly detection.
At 8:47 PM on a Friday night, the university's emergency monitoring system receives an encrypted message from inside the laboratory. Seconds later, the laboratory's monitoring dashboard goes offline. Several server records become unavailable, a prototype sensor network begins reporting impossible readings, and Dr. Arjun cannot be located.
When investigators enter the laboratory, they find a damaged workstation, an empty equipment cabinet, a missing encrypted storage device, and a handwritten note containing the phrase:
"The signal was never lost. Someone changed what it meant."
Initial investigators believe a physical intruder stole the research and escaped. However, a closer examination reveals inconsistencies: the access card records show an authorised entry, the camera system contains a gap, and an AI-generated suspect report points toward a student whose account may have been misused.
As the investigation expands, teams uncover evidence of earlier incidents involving the same laboratory: missing files, failed backups, altered sensor readings, unexplained account activity, damaged equipment, and reports that were quietly removed from the university's records.
The central mystery: Was Dr. Arjun the victim of a planned abduction, did he discover an insider's crime, or did someone deliberately stage the disappearance to conceal a much larger digital operation?
The answer should emerge from the evidence rather than being revealed at the beginning.
01. The Missing Researcher
Dr. Arjun Raman disappears after his final laboratory session. His office is locked, but his personal notebook remains open to a page describing an unknown signal.
Evidence: Notebook, visitor register, final calendar entry.
Question: When was he last reliably seen?
02. The Vanishing Research Files
Twenty-three research files are missing from the Project Phantom workstation. The file index lists their names, but the corresponding files cannot be found.
Evidence: Directory listing, file manifest, backup inventory.
03. The Damaged Workstation
A workstation is found damaged near the laboratory entrance. The damage appears dramatic, but the forensic image suggests that the last recorded activity occurred earlier.
Evidence: Device inspection sheet, file timestamps, disk image summary.
04. The Empty USB Locker
A USB device registered to the laboratory is missing. The inventory system records its return, but the receiving signature does not match the technician's usual signature.
Evidence: Inventory register, sign-in sheet, device serial number.
05. The Silent Server
The research file server stops responding at 8:41 PM. Its hardware health record shows no clear power failure, while its application logs stop abruptly.
Evidence: Server event log, UPS record, service status report.
06. The Encrypted Message
A short encoded message is recovered from a local terminal. Its contents refer to a time, a location, and the phrase "second key."
Evidence: Text file, encoding reference card, terminal history.
07. The Impossible Entry
An access card records an entry at 8:42 PM, but the card's owner claims to have left campus at 8:05 PM.
Evidence: Access log, departure register, witness statement.
08. The Missing Camera Minutes
The laboratory camera archive is missing six minutes of footage. The adjacent hallway camera remains operational.
Evidence: Camera indexes, adjacent-camera timestamps, maintenance records.
09. The Disconnected Sensor
A door sensor reports that the laboratory remained closed, while the access-control system records an entry.
Evidence: Sensor trace, RFID log, circuit inspection card.
10. The Last Email
Dr. Arjun receives an email warning him not to access Project Phantom. The displayed sender is a university executive, but the sender address is unfamiliar.
Evidence: Fictional email header, sender address, message timestamps.

Act III — The electronic crime scene
Cases 21–30 · Intermediate · ECE, CSE and cybersecurity investigation
21. The Impossible Temperature
A temperature sensor reports a sudden change that should be physically impossible under the laboratory's documented conditions.
Evidence: Temperature CSV, reference calibration chart, sampling interval.
Challenge: Distinguish a sensor malfunction from a manipulated record.
22. The Phantom Device
A fictional embedded device appears in a device inventory despite having no approved installation record.
Evidence: Device IDs, firmware manifest, connection timestamps.
Challenge: Determine which records associate the device with the lab incident.
23. The Missing Five Minutes
The sensor controller's log contains a five-minute gap, while the central monitoring system records uninterrupted activity.
Evidence: Two timestamped signal traces and controller event records.
Challenge: Correlate the clocks and identify the period that cannot be verified.
24. The Burnt Controller
A sensor controller is found damaged. Its maintenance history shows an inspection shortly before the incident.
Evidence: Component inventory, maintenance notes, fault codes.
Challenge: Establish what the records prove about the device's condition before the incident.
25. The Duplicate Access Card
Two access events use the same fictional card identifier at locations that should not be reachable within the recorded time interval.
Evidence: Access-control events, door locations and walking-time chart.
Challenge: Find the timeline contradiction without assuming that the card owner is guilty.
26. The Disconnected Network Link
A network monitoring record shows a brief loss of connectivity. The switch's event log suggests a different start time.
Evidence: Switch log, network monitoring graph, clock offset notes.
Challenge: Reconcile the timestamps and decide whether the outage explains the missing records.
27. The Fragmented Research File
A damaged research file can be partially reconstructed from a supplied evidence bundle.
Evidence: File fragments, format signature reference, SHA-256 manifest.
Challenge: Recover the relevant fragment and verify its integrity.
28. The Restart That Never Happened
A server status report says the machine restarted, but the event log contains no corresponding boot record.
Evidence: Uptime summary, operating-system events, monitoring history.
Challenge: Identify which record is insufficient or inconsistent and state what additional evidence is needed.
29. The Unregistered Storage Device
A device is found in the evidence inventory but does not appear in the authorised equipment register.
Evidence: Inventory, device metadata, file manifest.
Challenge: Establish whether the device could contain a relevant copy of Project Phantom.
30. The Second Key
A second key identifier appears in a recovered note. It conflicts with the key version listed in the recovery documentation.
Evidence: Key registry, note fragment, recovery log.

How Act IV changes the investigation
The university's AI system identifies Nila as the most suspicious person because her account appears in several anomalous events. The initial report describes the result as a high-confidence match.
However, participants discover that:
The model was trained using an incomplete dataset.
Some records were incorrectly labelled.
The account identifier was treated as if it proved the identity of the person using the account.
The model's audit history is incomplete.
Independent evidence does not establish that Nila operated the workstation.
Act V — The insider conspiracy
Cases 41–50 · Advanced · Multi-source investigation and final reconstruction
These final ten incidents should reveal the connections between the earlier cases. Each one gives the investigators another piece of the larger puzzle.
41. Project Phantom's Hidden Archive
A previously unknown research archive appears in an old inventory. Its manifest lists files missing from the main server.
Evidence: Archive manifest, file hashes, historical inventory.
Reveal: Some missing research existed in a separate location before the disappearance.
42. The Administrator's Shadow
A privileged account appears in several records where the account owner claims no activity occurred.
Evidence: Authentication history, approved maintenance windows, session records.
Reveal: Privileged access must be correlated with other evidence before it can be attributed to a person.
43. The Unsigned Order
A maintenance instruction authorises a temporary monitoring shutdown, but the approving signature cannot be verified.
Evidence: Approval register, document metadata, staff roster.
Reveal: The monitoring interruption may have been authorised using a false or incomplete approval trail.
44. The Two Timelines
The official incident report and the independently reconstructed timeline differ by several minutes.
Evidence: All prior timestamps, documented clock offsets, access records.
Reveal: A key event was placed in the wrong order in the original report.
45. The Backup That Was Never Tested
The university's official recovery report claims the research was recoverable. A supplied test log shows that the relevant files were never included in the last verified restoration test.
Evidence: Backup manifest, recovery checklist, test results.
Reveal: The institution's recovery assurance was based on incomplete evidence.
46. The Duplicate Research Copy
A recovered file matches an earlier version of Project Phantom but has a different timestamp and filename.
Evidence: File hashes, version history, archive manifest.
Reveal: A copy of the research may have existed outside the main server before the apparent theft.
47. Dr. Arjun's Final Note
A note recovered from a fictional storage locker explains why Arjun began investigating irregularities in Project Phantom.
Evidence: Notebook fragment, document provenance, earlier incident reports.
Reveal: Arjun had identified inconsistencies before his disappearance, but the note does not establish who took him.
48. The Accomplice Question
Several records connect Karan's maintenance actions with the suspicious time window, but none directly proves that he knowingly participated in the theft.
Evidence: Maintenance records, statements, access timeline.
Reveal: Teams must distinguish negligence, opportunity and deliberate participation.
49. The Final Signal
The recovered fragments form a message that refers to an evidence archive and a time window. The message is only useful when combined with the independently verified logs.
Evidence: Encoded note, archive manifest, corrected timeline.
Reveal: Teams identify the final evidence bundle that links the missing research to the privileged session.
50. The Last Signal — Final Case Report
All teams must reconstruct the incident, identify the best-supported culprit, explain the technical failures, evaluate the AI false lead, and recommend safeguards.
Evidence: Selected artifacts from all previous cases.
Reveal: The final answer must explain both the crime and how the investigation initially went wrong.
